Badger's End is an open-world D&D sandbox — built for 5e (and compatible with 5.5e / 2024 rules). A mysterious inn sits at the heart of a living city, designed so that different DMs and players can jump in and out of the world through independent sessions or connected arcs. Sessions are shaped by rolled events, random chaos, and a rotating cast of NPCs, so that no two nights at the inn are the same.

The Core Concept

Unlike a traditional campaign where one DM leads a single continuous adventure, Badger's End is built for rotation. Any DM at the table can run a night — a one-shot mystery, a two-session heist, or a chapter in a longer arc. When they hand back the world, the next DM inherits it. Players can come and go too; the inn is always here, and there's always a reason someone new might walk through the door.

The result is a shared world that keeps developing between sessions, whether or not any single DM is running. NPCs remember the party. Factions notice when their allies drift. Threads that one DM plants may not pay off for months, when another DM picks them up.

Opening Scene — the world of Badger's End in fifteen seconds.

The Setting

The Inn. Badger's End is a three-story tavern run by Mr. H, a Satyr bartender who left the Fae Wild for reasons he won't discuss. The common room is neutral ground for every faction in the city. The basement holds more than storage. The upper floor's rooms rarely have the same guest twice.

The City of Vellmere. Six districts, each with its own tone and its own secrets — the magical & alchemical district, the barracks and fighting halls, the town hall, the sanctuary and its graveyard, the harbor with its hidden smuggler's den, and the artisan market.

Beyond Vellmere. Past the city walls, roads wind through woods and past ruins that older maps don't always agree on.

The Factions

Six competing powers shape everything in the city. No single one of them fully controls it.

The ShieldCity guard, law, order, protection.
The LightTemple, faith, healing, public goodwill.
The CoinMerchant guild, trade, wealth, the port.
The QuillArcane circle, knowledge, magical law.
The ShadowSmugglers, crime, information brokers.
The MaskedThe hidden council pulling strings.

Players earn renown — or resentment — with each faction, and allegiances have consequences. The inn itself is neutral ground, respected by all.

The Cast

Anchor NPCs are permanent fixtures every DM plays consistently — Mr. H, Mr. Hoots the ever-watching owl, Captain Wren, Governor Ashvale, Sister Corvin, and others. They grow and change, but only through what actually happens at the table.

Session NPCs come from a randomized generator that spins up new patrons, contacts, and complications each night. When one earns a place in the group's memory, they can be promoted to anchor status.

DM-Owned NPCs belong to a specific DM's story arc — recurring villains, allies, rivals, or patrons that only appear when that DM is running. They're not shared with other DMs and stay tied to their creator's adventures. This lets each DM build their own persistent cast without stepping on anyone else's canon.

How a Session Works

Before the Session. The DM reviews the current World State — active threads, notable NPCs, faction tensions — and rolls the world alive. A d100 Events table sets what's happening in the city, at the inn, and among the factions. An optional d100 Chaos table adds a wild card if the DM wants to shake things up. The DM also decides which of their own recurring NPCs (if any) will appear tonight.

Opening the Scene. Most sessions begin at the inn — or wherever the party left off. The DM sets the mood using the rolled state (weather, city event, atmosphere at the inn) and presents an opening hook. Players describe how their PCs arrive, return, or wake up in one of the upstairs rooms. Returning players pick up whatever renown, gear, and relationships they left with.

The Sandbox in Play. Players choose their own path — chase the hook, follow up on a lead from a previous session, seek out an anchor NPC, or wander into any of the six districts to find their own trouble. They can also check the optional Hero's Board in the common room, where NPCs, guilds, and factions post quests, bounties, and pleas for help; postings can persist between sessions or appear fresh, giving the party a self-serve menu of adventures when they want one. Anchor NPCs are always available; randomized patrons fill any new encounters. Every choice can move renown with one faction or another — and every faction is watching.

Handling Continuity. Sessions can stand alone (a single-night mystery, a bar brawl gone strange) or connect into longer arcs run by the same DM across multiple nights. Different DMs can trade off in either mode — picking up loose threads, or starting fresh alongside them. Players are welcome to bring their PCs into anyone's session; the world keeps track of who they are.

Wrapping Up. Before the group breaks, the DM logs the session: threads opened or resolved, renown gained or lost, notable events for the World Event Log, and any promoted NPCs. This handoff is what makes the sandbox work — the next DM inherits a living world, not a blank page.

Between Sessions. Time passes in-world. Factions make quiet moves. The next session might be the same DM continuing an arc, or a different DM starting something new. Player characters carry everything with them: their renown, their reputation, and the memory of the regulars who remember them right back.

Where It Lives

Primary play is on a self-hosted Foundry VTT server, with maps made in Inkarnate and imported as playable scenes with grids, walls, and lighting. Every asset is also designed to work in person — print-ready maps, NPC cards, and quick-reference sheets — so a session can move to a physical table without losing anything.

What Makes It Different

Traditional campaigns are the DM's story. Badger's End is a place — one that keeps being interesting whether or not any particular story is running. Players return to the inn between adventures. The world moves on without them. Small choices come back around. It's less like a novel, more like a neighborhood.

The living cast of Badger's End. Established anchors are fully written and played consistently across DMs. Story anchors have profiles waiting to be filled in with a name, race, and specifics at the table when they first appear.

Mr. H
Head Bartender — heart of the Badger's End
Established

RaceSatyr (Fae Wild origin) MysteryWhy did he stay?
Warm, unhurried, and almost impossible to rattle. Mr. H remembers every regular's drink, notices when someone is off, and has a gift for making strangers feel like they've been coming here for years. He deflects personal questions with a laugh, a refilled glass, or a well-timed change of subject — never coldly, never obviously. He simply doesn't go there.

The City

Vellmere
A small port city where all six factions meet
All Factions
A small port city built at the mouth of the sea. Half-timbered buildings, cobblestone streets, arched stone gates opening toward a harbor of tall ships. Home to six factions, each with their own district and their own interests, and one tavern where they all end up sooner or later.

The Six Districts

Magic & Alchemy District
Scholars, wizards, and apothecaries
The Quill
A twilight lane of tall crooked buildings — apothecary and enchanter shopfronts with painted signs, bay windows crowded with colored glass bottles, dried herbs hanging from the eaves, a wisp of violet smoke drifting from a chimney. The quiet part of the city, where knowledge is currency and nothing is quite what it appears on the label.
Cast. Aldric Fenn spends most of his research hours in these lanes when he isn't at the Badger's End.
Barracks & Fighting District
Where the city's guards train and off-duty soldiers gather
The Shield
A stone garrison courtyard — practice dummies, weapon racks along one wall, a small smithy at the far end throwing sparks. Guards spar in surcoats while off-duty soldiers watch and heckle from a low wall. The Shield's daily rhythm, in flagstone and steel.
Cast. Captain Wren's patrol district begins and ends here.
Town Hall District
The seat of civic authority
The Shield
A small cobbled square with a modest timber-and-stone town hall — steep slate roof, shuttered windows with flower boxes, a little bell tower on top, weathered wooden doors flanked by a bench. An old tree beside the square, a small stone well at the center. Quaint from the outside; where the city's important decisions get made on the inside.
Cast. Governor Ashvale keeps offices here.
Sanctuary & Graveyard District
The temple and the resting place
The Light
A modest stone sanctuary with an arched wooden door, a small graveyard beside it under old crooked trees, wayside shrines with burning candles along the cobbled path to the entrance. A place for the faithful, the mourning, and those who need somewhere quiet to sit with something heavy.
Cast. Sister Corvin tends both the temple and the congregation the city forgets about.
Harbor & Smuggler's Den
The working port and the shadowed alleys behind it
The Shadow
A working port at dusk — tall ships tied up at wooden docks, coiled ropes and stacked crates, gulls wheeling overhead, warehouses and dockside taverns crowding the water's edge. Shadowy alleys lead off between them. Legitimate business by day. Something else after dark.
Presence. The Shadow's territory. The Masked have been seen here more than once — always at hours when no one is quite sure whether they saw them.
Market & Artisans District
The bright, bustling heart of trade
The Coin
A bustling market square — stalls of adventuring gear, produce, and textiles under colorful awnings, artisan shopfronts (blacksmith, tailor, jeweler) around the edges, merchants calling their wares, adventurers browsing, children running between stalls. The most alive part of the city on any given day.
Cast. Mira Kael's shop is here, tucked between the Golden Thread Tailor and the corner of the Oaken Hammer's forge.
